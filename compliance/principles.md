# Compliance principles

**Status: normative within the fiction. Illustrative, not verified.**

The cross-cutting rules that apply to all customer-facing content, whatever the surface.

## 1. Transparency and fairness

- Present the real offer plainly. No misleading, exaggerated, or half-true copy (Unfair
  Commercial Practices Directive).
- Every cost, risk, condition, and limit that affects a decision appears **before** the
  person commits, not after.
- No dark patterns: no pre-ticked boxes, no disguised costs, no pressure, no harder path
  to decline than to accept (Distance Marketing of Financial Services).

## 2. Prohibited claims

Never write copy that:

- Promises or implies guaranteed returns, profit, or a "risk-free" outcome.
- Calls a product "free" when conditions or costs apply.
- Hides or downplays fees, risks, or the total amount payable.
- Guarantees approval, or states deposit-protection terms not confirmed in
  `disclosures.md`.

## 3. Plain language and accessibility

- Write so anyone understands on the first read; expand acronyms, for example KYC (Know
  Your Customer). See `../voice-and-tone/voice.md`.
- Meet the accessibility obligations in `accessibility.md` (this is law, not a nicety).

## 4. Timing

- Disclose pre-contractual information before sign-up, contract terms at agreement, and
  ongoing information (statements, changes) on time.
- Give notice of changes to terms or fees within the required period before they apply.

## 5. Evidence and consent

- Where consent is required, it is freely given, specific, and informed, and it is as
  easy to withdraw as to give (see `data-privacy.md`).
- Keep a record of what was shown and agreed, and when.

## 6. Precedence and the safety rule

- This layer overrides voice, patterns, and components (see `/CLAUDE.md`).
- If a string touches money, risk, credit, deposits, identity, or data and no rule here
  clearly covers it, mark it `[NEEDS COMPLIANCE REVIEW]` and do not invent the wording.

## Eval hooks

- No prohibited claim terms ("guaranteed", "risk-free", "free" without conditions).
- Costs and risks appear before the commit action, not only after.
- Decline is no harder than accept; no pre-ticked consent.
