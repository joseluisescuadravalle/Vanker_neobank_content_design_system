# Identity and KYC

**Status: normative within the fiction. Illustrative, not verified.**

How Vanker talks about identity verification and anti-money-laundering checks (AMLR /
AMLA). The rules are strict; the tone stays plain, calm, and never accusatory.

## Verifying identity (onboarding)

- Explain in plain language what is needed, why, and roughly how long it takes. Use
  "confirm your identity", not the raw term KYC (Know Your Customer).
- Do: "To keep your money safe and meet the law, we need to confirm your identity. Have
  your ID card ready. It takes about two minutes."
- Accept and explain electronic identification, including the EU Digital Identity Wallet
  (eIDAS2), where offered.

## Ongoing checks and extra information

- When more information is needed later (for example source of funds), explain why, and
  what happens if it is not provided, without implying wrongdoing.
- Do: "We need to ask about a recent payment. This is a routine check that all banks
  must do. It helps keep your account secure."
- Not: "Your account is suspicious. Justify this transaction."

## Restrictions, holds, and blocks

- When an account or payment is restricted, be factual and minimal. The law can limit
  what can be disclosed (no "tipping off"), so do not speculate on the reason.
- Always give a route to support and, where possible, an expected timeframe.
- Do: "We have paused this while we run a routine check. We will update you within [time].
  You can contact us any time."

## Data minimization

- Ask only for the data the check needs; do not collect or echo more (ties to
  `data-privacy.md`).

## Eval hooks

- Identity copy explains the why and gives a time estimate.
- No accusatory language in verification or restriction copy.
- Restriction copy gives a support route and, where possible, a timeframe.
